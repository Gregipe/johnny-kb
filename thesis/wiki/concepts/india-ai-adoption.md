# India AI Adoption

- First filed: 2026-11-23 (from sources/db-who-wins-ai-india.md)

## Definition

The thesis that India's AI win condition is mass adoption of commoditised intelligence on pre-built digital rails (UPI/JAM), applied to enormous unmet needs in healthcare, education, credit and agriculture — not frontier-model invention. Short form: "you don't need to invent it."

## Why it matters to Greg

1. It independently confirms the application-layer thesis: value accrues to those who apply cheap intelligence to expensive problems, not to model inventors. Hunar sits precisely in the report's adopters layer (banks, healthcare, consumer discretionary). (2026-11-23)
2. The report's flagship case — an Indian bank's AI collections system speaking to customers in their own language, optimising channel/time/tone, materially better recoveries with an unchanged team — is a Hunar-shaped product already live in the wild. Proof of demand for vernacular AI on existing workflows. (2026-11-23)
3. The SMB adoption gap (23% vs 43% US) + MSME credit gap (~25% of demand) + "price-sensitive market needs a crystal-clear business case" = the exact pain Hunar's ₹2/min economics addresses. (2026-11-23)
4. Portfolio angle: India as anti-AI diversifier — near-zero Nifty IT correlation to Nasdaq, 19× vs 40× P/E — supports index exposure in Greg's book as a hedge to AI concentration. (2026-11-23)
5. Sovereignty angle: a model can be banned without warning by the US; India's local lightweight models + "whole world's data in India" posture create a trust/governance moat that frontier labs can't claim. (2026-11-23)

## Key mechanics

- Trust outranks capability: India #1 globally in AI trust and intentional work use — the adoption rail is cultural, not just technical.
- Digital public infrastructure (JAM, UPI, eKYC) lets AI deploy without new rails — the marginal cost of deployment is the lowest of any large market.
- Government facilitation: IndiaAI ($1.1B, 10K GPUs), three local on-device language models (Nov 2026), Group A GovTech rating.
- Constraint stack: power (26GW AI load by 2032), compute (<0.5% of global GPU clusters), capital ($4bn private AI investment), data-centre buildout concentrated in Mumbai/Hyderabad.

## Open questions / to watch

- Can 23% firm adoption converge toward the US's 43%? The gap is the opportunity — and the report's own risk case.
- Does entry-level hiring moderation dry up the senior pipeline? Slow-burn risk flagged by DB; connects to the fresher-crisis research.
- Will the anti-AI hedge hold once US AI sentiment stabilises? DB itself flags the unwind risk.
