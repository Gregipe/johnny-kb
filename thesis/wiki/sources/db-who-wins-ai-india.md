## Thesis

Adoption beats invention. India's AI win condition is deploying off-the-shelf models on pre-built digital rails against enormous unmet needs — healthcare, education, credit, agriculture — not building frontier models. Report also argues India equities are a short-term anti-AI diversifier with near-zero correlation to AI-heavy indices.

## Key claims (dated)

- 2026-11: India ranks #3 globally on Stanford HAI AI Vibrancy; #1 in AI trust and intentional use at work (Melbourne/KPMG); ChatGPT's largest market by weekly users.
- 2026-11: Six bull reasons — demographics (1.5B, median 29), digital rails (UPI/JAM), uniquely suited problems (access > productivity), workforce scale (11M grads/yr), government facilitation (IndiaAI, 3 local lightweight models), buy-don't-invent leverage vs US/China.
- 2026-11: Four bear risks — services disruption (partially offset: only 1% of workforce, record openings), usage ≠ productivity (23% firm adoption vs 43% US), power/compute/capital constraints, regulation + protectionist public sentiment.
- 2026-11: Investment thesis: near-zero 1-yr correlation Nifty IT vs Nasdaq/KOSPI; record $9.5bn November inflows; Nifty IT at 19× P/E vs Nasdaq ~40×.

## Data points

- 500M people lifted out of poverty 2011-2023; extreme poverty 2.6%.
- 70% internet penetration; ~90% bank account ownership.
- 153 data centres (14th globally); 1.6→6GW by 2029; ~1/3 Mumbai, 1/4 Hyderabad.
- Private AI investment: India $4bn vs US $285bn, China $12bn (2025).
- MSME credit gap ~25% of demand (SIDBI).
- AI DC power load forecast 26GW by 2031-32 (~7% of peak demand).
- Reliance $110bn/7yr, Adani $100bn, Tata-OpenAI 100MW→1GW; $67.5bn foreign commitments.
- ICRIER 650-firm survey: no apocalyptic job losses; moderation concentrated at entry level.
