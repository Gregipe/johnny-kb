# The Commanding Heights (digest)

Thesis: "The Commanding Heights are scarce, controllable positions of economic power whose holders durably capture the gains of increasingly capable and diffused intelligence." (Lenin's 1922 phrase, repurposed.)

Key sections:
- Overture: intelligence is the giveaway/loss leader; margins accrue to the conduit. "One either owns the rails or pays those who do." Gutenberg, Bell Labs, railroads pattern: wealth settles far from the originary point.
- The Line: capex/index/capability are one line ($800B→$1T+ hyperscaler capex); training costs +3.5x/yr; token prices -47%/quarter; sovereign backstops the line.
- The World to Come: K-shaped economy; first casualties = rich world's back office (Ravi the Bangalore IIT grad; TCS headcount -23,460 vs +7.5% operating profit; 16 net tech jobs per 100 Indian graduates); emigration paths closing; licensing ("the Guild") protects incumbents, freezes out youth.
- The Commanding Heights (Part VI): durability test — scarce + controllable + entrenched by the activity it enables. Fleeting bottlenecks vs Heights; margins migrate (power→transmission→chips). Sovereign takes its toll (antitrust).
- The Firm (Part VII): fourth settlement — "firms composed of a few operators, paid and governed like capital, directing work done mostly by models."
- Neo-Dollar (Part IV): stablecoins on messaging rails; "manufacture dollar demand and intermediate its transactions."
- Towers of Ivory: concierge medicine, at-home tutors, elite schools, private security = durable K-top demand in fixed supply.
- Closing: "Sell your Heights while they remain so"; most firms are coal for the furnace; distribution and network owners win.
