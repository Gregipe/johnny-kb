# The AI Capability Gap in Indian MSMEs/SMBs: What They Use AI For, and Where They Need Help

**Report date:** 18 November 2026 (all sources retrieved and verified on this date)
**Scope:** Follow-up to *Indian SMB Structural Needs & Anxieties 2024–2026* (§4.2), which found 84% of surveyed Indian/APAC small businesses using AI while 86% say they need more help to use it well, and 46.2% of MSMEs fully offline. This report double-clicks both findings. Grounded in Indian institutional surveys (CII–ISB, India SME Forum, NASSCOM, IAMAI–Kantar, SIDBI–Crisil), vendor SMB surveys (Meta/YouGov, Google×India SME Forum, Salesforce, GoDaddy), and analyst studies (Dun & Bradstreet, RIS). Every load-bearing figure was checked against the primary document or the reporting page; definitional conflicts and weak samples are flagged inline.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

- **The dominant AI need-statement is demand-side: "help me get found, chosen, and answered by customers."** Across every survey with India data, the top actual uses of AI are content creation for marketing, advertising, and customer communication — not accounting, inventory, or compliance. Strongest citation: Google×India SME Forum (July 2026, n=3,249 Indian MSMEs): 34% already use AI for content generation (the #1 stated use) and 26% for automation tools; CII–ISB (Oct 2026, n=2,919): business communication is the #1 AI use at 34.9%, ahead of content creation (22.3%).
- **Adoption is broad but astonishingly shallow.** Depending on definition, "AI use" runs 65.8%–84%, yet Dun & Bradstreet (Oct 2026) finds only 5% of Indian MSMEs have AI core to operations (24% no adoption, 35% informal experimenting, 37% automating selected tasks), and Vi Business (Jun 2026) finds only 25% have integrated AI into workflows even though 57% call it vital.
- **The "86% need more help" gap decomposes into, ranked: (1) skills/training and hands-on implementation support — the single biggest barrier by every survey; (2) cost and ROI clarity — 51% cite cost as the leading barrier to AI *training*; (3) data/system readiness — half cannot measure AI's ROI and only 21% of AI-using MSMEs have any ERP or business software underneath.** Trust/privacy, vernacular support, and scheme awareness compound these.
- **The offline gap is a perception-and-capability problem more than a connectivity one:** 46.2% of MSMEs remain fully offline (India SME Forum/Meta, Dec 2025, n=7,835 — convenience sample, "indicative"), and among non-users of digital tools, ~46% say they simply don't need them (RIS). Meanwhile 97.3% are unaware of any government digitalisation scheme.
- **Language is a silent first-order barrier:** 98% of India's ~870M internet users access content in Indic languages and 57% of urban users prefer Indic over English (IAMAI–Kantar ICUBE 2024), yet MSME training is "often generic, English-centric" (ISF/Meta) and commercial activities (search, shopping) lag entertainment in local-language usage.

## 2. What SMBs Actually Use AI For Today

**The dominant need-statement: AI for customer acquisition and engagement — creating marketing content, running local advertising, and handling customer conversations.** The job it does is demand generation for businesses with no marketing department: producing posts, product descriptions and ads cheaply, and answering customer queries at WhatsApp speed and scale.

The evidence, strongest first:

- **Google×India SME Forum, "The Google Dividend" (July 2026; survey of 3,249 Indian MSMEs — 72% micro, 24% small, 4% medium; majority Tier-2/3):** 34% already use AI for **content generation** (top stated use) and 26% for automation tools; 19% report productivity gains from AI. *(Primary PDF verified.)*
- **CII–ISB, "MSMEs in India" (Oct 2026; analytical sample 2,919 MSMEs across all 36 states/UTs, 40 sectors; voluntary, not probability-weighted):** AI is used primarily for **business communication (34.9%)**, then **content creation (22.3%)** and training (18.9%). *(Verified via Business Standard, 13 Oct 2026.)*
- **Meta-commissioned YouGov APAC study (fieldwork 6 Aug–2 Sep 2026; n=3,983 owners, 1–49 employees, 9 markets incl. India):** among past-12-month AI users, **51% used it to create or edit content**, 43% for local advertising and marketing, 38% for translation, 37% for customer support and communications. *Caveat: no India-only breakout published — figures are 9-market aggregates.* *(Primary page verified.)*
- **Salesforce 6th SMB Trends Report (Dec 2024; n=3,350 across 26 countries, 200 in India):** 78% of Indian SMBs using or experimenting with AI (vs 75% global); India's top-3 use cases: **automated service chatbots, marketing campaign optimisation, generating new content** (ranked; no percentages).
- The customer-conversation half is WhatsApp-shaped: Kantar (2025, cited by Meta) found **91% of online adults in India chat with a business weekly**; Meta launched Business AI on WhatsApp for Indian small businesses in May 2026.

What they are *not* yet using AI for, at scale: back-office depth. For most, AI is a general-purpose assistant doing individual tasks — 84% of AI-using MSMEs rely primarily on general-purpose assistants rather than AI embedded in business systems (D&B; report exhibit).

**Adoption-rate spread (definitions matter, cite with care):** 84% "currently use AI" (Meta/YouGov, APAC) → 76% "use in some form" (D&B, India) → 78% "using or experimenting" (Salesforce India) → 65.8% "use AI tools" (CII–ISB) → 25% "integrated into workflows" (Vi Business, insights from 2.5 lakh+ MSMEs) → **5% "core to operations" (D&B)**. A plausible reading: roughly two-thirds of Indian MSMEs have touched AI; a quarter use it in any real workflow; a twentieth run on it.

One common misread to avoid: the NASSCOM–Meta whitepaper's 94% "acknowledge AI's growth potential" and 68% "new products & services" (Jun 2024; ~220 tech-enabled MSMEs) measure **belief among a digitally-mature sample**, not deployment.

## 3. The Help Gap — What "86% Need More Help" Concretely Means

The Meta/YouGov finding is precise: **86% of owners say small businesses need more guidance to gain the full benefits of AI** (n=3,983, incl. India), and the support they say they'd value most is **clearer information on cost and ROI** and **guidance on data privacy and security**. Breaking "help" into named categories, ranked by weight of evidence:

**3.1 Skills, training, and implementation support (the biggest gap).**
- India SME Forum/Meta (Dec 2025, n=7,835): **"navigating and setting up digital tools" is the single hardest challenge in the entire survey — 36.8% find it somewhat/very challenging (11.1% "very")** — versus 5.3–8.0% for finding customers, entering new markets, hiring, or securing finance. The report names this **"the implementation gap"**: the bottleneck is choosing and deploying tools, not awareness or capital. (The report's highlights page inconsistently states 52.6% for tool-finding difficulty vs 35.6% in the body — flagged.)
- Google×ISF (Jul 2026): **75% cite skill gaps** — the top AI-adoption barrier. NASSCOM–Meta (Jun 2024): 65% cannot leverage AI for **lack of awareness of tools/resources** (their #1 challenge), 57% lack technical expertise, 72% call for training programmes.
- D&B (Oct 2026): 70.8% say training would accelerate adoption, yet only 15% reinvest AI gains in people/skills (report exhibits).

**3.2 Affordability and ROI clarity.** Cost is the **leading barrier to AI training, cited by 51%** (Meta/YouGov 2026); the most-valued support is "clearer information on cost and return on investment." Google×ISF: 60% cite budget limitations; NASSCOM–Meta: 59% cite budget constraints and 91% believe AI should be "democratically available." Counterpoint worth noting: D&B finds the constraint shifting from affordability to execution — 60% now say AI is within reach for a firm their size.

**3.3 Data readiness, integration, and measurement (why value doesn't materialise).**
- **CII–ISB (Oct 2026): 65.8% of MSMEs use AI tools, but only 21% use ERP or customised business software** — AI layered on top of no system of record.
- Google×ISF: **50% struggle to measure AI effectiveness and ROI**; 48% cite organisational readiness. D&B: AI outputs increasingly drive decisions but only ~17% have formal guardrails. ISF/Meta: analytics usage is 13.2% even among the digitised cohort.
- Vi Business (Jun 2026): 57% view AI as vital; only 25% have integrated it — the trying-vs-value gap in one number.

**3.4 Trust, privacy, and security.** 56.4% of MSMEs raise data-privacy/security concerns (NASSCOM–Meta); privacy/security guidance is among the two most-valued supports (Meta/YouGov). Regulation compounds it: **only 1.4% of MSMEs are aware of DPDP Act guidelines; 52.4% explicitly unaware** (ISF/Meta).

**3.5 Vernacular support.** See §4.2 — training is "generic, English-centric" (ISF/Meta), and vernacular/customer-facing AI deployment sits near single digits (D&B: ~9%, report exhibits).

**3.6 Scheme and compliance navigation.** **97.3% of surveyed MSMEs are unaware of any government digitalisation scheme or incentive** (ISF/Meta, n=7,835; corroborated by ETGovernment, 2 Dec 2025). CII–ISB: 74.9% know of at least one government scheme but only 42.6% use one. NASSCOM–Meta: 52% want help building legally compliant businesses from the start.

**3.7 Sector-specific proof and peer support.** 45% of MSMEs want industry-specific use cases; **78% would implement AI within three quarters if adequate peer support existed** (NASSCOM–Meta). D&B: 53% cite proof from similar firms as the key accelerator.

## 4. The Offline and Language Barrier

**4.1 The 46.2% offline — and what keeps them there.** India SME Forum/Meta's *State of Digitalisation in Indian MSMEs* (Dec 2025; n=7,835 across 28 states/UTs; convenience sample, self-described as "indicative rather than statistically representative"; 59.1% micro, 73% male-owned) found **53.8% (4,218) have adopted at least one digital tool; 46.2% operate fully offline**. Directionally corroborated: D&B's 2026 report repeats the figure, and the Economic Survey 2024-25 (cited within the ISF report) estimates 55–60% of registered MSMEs online in some form; NASSCOM–Deloitte's 2025 index puts digitally-engaged micro/small at 57%.

The report gives no quantified reason breakdown for the 46.2% (a genuine data gap), but its barrier analysis plus the RIS survey converge on four causes:
1. **Perceived irrelevance** — RIS (*MSME Digitalisation in India*; sample size unconfirmed): among digital non-users, **~46% believe their business doesn't need digital tech**; ISF/Meta: 17.1% of all respondents rate digital "not important."
2. **Digital-literacy/skills deficit** — 47.7% find setup and day-to-day use of digital platforms challenging (ISF/Meta); IAMAI–Kantar ICUBE 2023 found the offline population's leading self-reported barriers are "too difficult to understand and use" and "not aware of the benefits," ahead of affordability or devices.
3. **Cost/finance** — upfront device/connectivity/licence costs; collateral-based lending excludes micro and women-led units (ISF/Meta, qualitative).
4. **Trust/security** — fear of fraud and data leakage; 97.3% unaware of schemes including cybersecurity support.

The stakes are quantified: among the digitised 53.8%, **41.4% saw a 21–30% sales increase** and ~70% gained ≥10% new customers (ISF/Meta).

**4.2 The vernacular dimension.** Consumer India has already gone Indic: **~870 million internet users — 98% of all users — accessed the internet in Indic languages in 2024, and 57% of urban users prefer Indic over English** (IAMAI–Kantar ICUBE 2024; 2025 wave: 958M users, 38% of the population still offline, chiefly for lack of interest/awareness). ~140M use voice commands; 44% of users now engage AI-powered features (voice/image search, chatbots) — ICUBE 2024/2025.

But commercial activity lags entertainment in local languages: in urban India, 81% watch videos in Indic languages but only **36% shop online and 23% search online in them** (ICUBE 2023) — and conversion is weakest precisely in major business languages (Gujarati, Marathi, Bengali) due to thin content. The classic baseline still holds directionally: Google–KPMG (2017) found Indian-language users (234M) overtook English users (175M) in 2016, **88% of Indian-language users are more likely to respond to a vernacular ad than an English one**, and 44% cited limited local-language enablement as a key limitation of digital services.

On the SMB side, the evidence is thinner but consistent: ISF/Meta states MSME training programmes are "**often generic, English-centric**, and scheduled at inconvenient times"; D&B's 2026 exhibits put customer-facing/vernacular AI adoption at ~9% (against 38% of APAC AI-using SMBs using AI for *translation* — Meta/YouGov); and ISF's recommendations centre on vernacular content, multilingual tools, and bilingual local facilitators. **No survey quantifies language as a driver of MSME offlineness specifically — the link is inferential and should be treated as such.**

## 5. Synthesis — What This Means for Anyone Building for Indian SMBs

1. **Sell customer-acquisition outcomes, not "AI."** The proven wedge is content/marketing/communication — the one job every SMB already pays for. Products that show "more customers per rupee" align with both the top use-case and the top requested help (cost/ROI clarity).
2. **The binding constraint is capability, not price or awareness.** Setup difficulty (36.8%) dwarfs finance difficulty (~5–8%) as a self-reported barrier. Done-for-you onboarding, sector templates, and human-assisted implementation are the product, not a go-to-market nicety. D&B's framing is exact: the constraint has shifted "from affordability to execution."
3. **AI arrived before the system of record.** 65.8% use AI tools while only 21% have ERP — most AI runs as general-purpose chat over unstructured data. The durable opportunity is boring plumbing: digitise the ledger/inventory/customer record first, then embed AI into it.
4. **Vernacular is table stakes, not a feature flag.** With 98% of users on Indic content and training English-centric, voice-first and Indic-first interfaces address the help gap and the offline gap simultaneously.
5. **Trust is a product problem.** With 1.4% DPDP awareness, ~17% guardrail adoption, and a live fraud wave (see prior report), bundled security/privacy assurances and plain-language compliance are differentiators.
6. **The 46.2% offline need a reason before a tool.** ~46% of digital non-users say they don't need digital tech — yet once firms cross the first hurdle, dependence accelerates sharply (>50% of adopters call digital mission-critical). Proof-from-peers (78% would adopt with peer support) is the cheapest conversion lever.
7. **Platform distribution works; government schemes don't (yet).** 97.3% scheme unawareness versus Meta/Google/WhatsApp tool ubiquity says the last mile is being built by platforms, not policy — validating CII–ISB's call for a unified scheme-discovery platform.
