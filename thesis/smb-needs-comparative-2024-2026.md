# Indian SMB Structural Needs & Anxieties: A Comparative Assessment, 2024 → 2025 → 2026

**Report date:** 18 November 2026 (all sources retrieved on this date)
**Scope:** Structural needs and anxieties of Indian small and medium businesses (MSMEs, kiranas, marketplace sellers, D2C brands, small fleets) across compliance, credit, payments, commerce channels, labor, energy, and fraud. Grounded in Indian institutional sources (GST Council/PIB, RBI, SIDBI, TransUnion CIBIL, NPCI, NCAER), practitioner platforms (ClearTax, ICAI) and business press. Where figures conflict or rest on weak sourcing, this is stated.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

- **The anxiety mix has rotated.** In 2024 the dominant anxieties were supply-side (credit access, payments-rail continuity, retrospective GST demands); by 2026 they are demand- and security-side: quick-commerce cannibalisation of kirana demand, an industrial-scale fraud wave, and new per-transaction digital costs.
- **GST anxiety peaked in 2024–25, then de-escalated sharply in late 2026.** Notice volume rose 33% to ~2 lakh in FY25, but the 56th Council (Sept 2025) simplified rates to 5%/18% and the 57th (Oct 2026) withdrew arrest powers and cut penalties. The coercive edge is receding; data-matching scrutiny is not.
- **Formal credit supply improved; the bottom of the pyramid got tighter.** MSME credit exposure grew 13–16% YoY with delinquencies at five-year lows, yet the credit gap held at ~₹30 lakh crore, new-to-credit share of originations fell 52%→42%, and stress concentrated in sub-₹10 lakh exposures.
- **Delayed payments remain a structural wound:** ~₹20,458 crore across ~87,525 MSME Samadhaan cases were still pending in October 2026, of ₹55,244 crore cumulatively claimed.
- **The labour-code shock landed 21 November 2025** and its cost consequences are still unfolding (ESIC re-scoping; EPFO wage ceiling ₹15,000→₹25,000 from 17 Sept 2026) — a compliance-cost step-change with no 2024 precedent.
- **Zero-cost digital rails are ending:** NPCI's first-ever UPI merchant fee (0.4% on transactions >₹2,000, announced Sept 2026, deferred after trader-body objections) closes the free-MDR era SMBs have enjoyed since 2020.
- **AI is now an aspiration-and-anxiety pair:** 84% of surveyed Indian/APAC small businesses use AI, but 86% say they need more help to use it well — a capability gap, not yet a cost line.

## 2. Needs/Anxieties That Have AMPLIFIED, 2024→2026

### 2.1 Quick-commerce displacement of kirana and small retail demand
- **What:** Fear that 10–30-minute delivery platforms (Blinkit, Zepto, Instamart, Flipkart Minutes, Amazon Now) are permanently diverting everyday retail demand from neighbourhood stores.
- **Evidence/magnitude:** Datum Intelligence (Oct 2024) found **67% of kirana owners reported lower sales since quick commerce arrived; 74% saw it as a long-term threat**. The market roughly doubled from ~$6.0B (CY2024) to ~$11.5B (CY2025, RedSeer) — analyst definitions conflict, but the direction is consistent. Industry-wide dark stores crossed **6,000 by April 2026** (Bernstein, via TechCrunch); Blinkit alone ~2,243 (Mar 2026).
- **Why it amplified:** Every major platform scaled at once, festive discounting intensified, and **no restrictive regulation materialised through 2026** despite CAIT's Nov 2024 white paper and the AICPDF's March 2025 CCI petition. (AICPDF's claim of ~2 lakh kirana closures in a year is a **lobbyist estimate, independently unverified**.)
- **Source:** Datum Intelligence, *State of Quick Commerce Market 2024* (Oct 2024) — https://datumintell.com/report-quick-commerce.html ; TechCrunch (11 Apr 2026) — https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/11/walmart-owned-flipkart-amazon-are-squeezing-indias-quick-commerce-startups ; ET LegalWorld (Mar 2025) — https://legal.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/corporate-business/probe-unfair-predatory-practices-by-quick-commerce-platforms-aicpdf-to-cci/114402966

### 2.2 Cyber-fraud, scam exposure, and "digital arrest" fear
- **What:** Direct losses (UPI collect-request scams, ransomware, business-email compromise) plus ambient fear that degrades trust in the digital rails SMBs now depend on.
- **Evidence/magnitude:** I4C data tabled in Parliament (Mar 2025): digital-arrest cases rose **60,676 (2023, ₹339 cr) → 1,23,672 (2024, ₹1,935.51 cr)**. Total reported cyber-fraud losses were **₹22,845.73 crore across 22.68 lakh complaints in CY2024**. PwC's Indian Payments Handbook 2025–30 puts UPI-specific fraud losses at **₹573 cr (FY23) → ~₹1,100 cr (FY24)**, cases 2.0M→2.4M, and Sophos's 2026 survey found 56% of hit Indian organisations paid ransoms. **Caution: datasets conflict** — ORF/NHRC-derived figures put digital-arrest losses above ₹22,495 crore in 2025, irreconcilable with I4C's 2024 number. Direction is unambiguous; magnitudes are not.
- **Why it amplified:** UPI volumes roughly doubled (avg ~14.3B/month in 2024 → 25.47B in Oct 2026), enlarging the attack surface; scams industrialised; the institutional response (a Supreme Court-ordered pan-India CBI probe, Dec 2025) itself signals severity.
- **Source:** I4C/Parliament data compiled by FrankonFraud (Jan 2026) — https://frankonfraud.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Digital-Arrest-Scams-Explained.pdf ; PwC India, *Indian Payments Handbook 2025–2030* (2025) — https://www.pwc.in/assets/pdfs/indian-payments-handbook-2025-2030.pdf ; Sophos *State of Ransomware 2026* — https://www.sophos.com/en-us/blog/sophos-state-of-ransomware-2026

### 2.3 System-triggered GST scrutiny and mismatch notices
- **What:** Machine-generated notices from GSTR-1 vs GSTR-3B mismatches (Rule 88C) and ITC claimed vs GSTR-2B (Rule 88D), with 7-day response windows and the risk of blocked returns.
- **Evidence/magnitude:** ClearTax's *State of Tax Assurance Report 2026* (via The Hindu, 8 May 2026) found GST notices rose **33.3% to ~2,00,000 in FY 2024-25**, with 8 of 10 notices system-triggered and ~35% involving ITC mismatches — though that survey covered large enterprises (>₹500 cr revenue); SMB-specific counts are **not found in official sources**. From 1 July 2025, returns became barred if filed more than three years late, and Section 74A demands for FY 2024-25 began landing in 2026.
- **Why it amplified:** The state built the machinery (IMS, GSTR-1A, biometric Aadhaar checks) and then used it. (The coercive component cooled after Oct 2026 — see §3.1; data-matching persists.)
- **Source:** The Hindu (8 May 2026) — https://www.thehindu.com/business/internal-mismatches-are-the-major-reason-behind-gst-notices-clear-tax/article70956492.ece ; A2Z Taxcorp (Jun 2025) — https://a2ztaxcorp.net/from-july-gst-returns-to-be-barred-after-three-year-window/

### 2.4 Delayed payments and the receivables squeeze
- **What:** Working capital stuck because large buyers pay MSMEs late — governed by the MSMED Act's 45-day rule, the s.43B(h) income-tax disallowance on buyers (from FY 2023-24), and half-yearly MSME-1 filings.
- **Evidence/magnitude:** MSME Samadhaan had cumulatively received **2,56,892 applications claiming ₹55,244 crore by 15 Oct 2025; ~87,525 cases worth ~₹20,458 crore were still pending as of Oct 2026**. A dated 2024 baseline for pending cases was not found, so a clean growth rate cannot be asserted — the caseload's persistence is the finding.
- **Why it amplified:** The 43B(h) tax lever made MSME payment timing a buyer-side tax issue (raising friction on both sides); new filings moved to the ODR portal from 15 Oct 2025 and the MSMED (Amendment) Act 2026 rewrote enforcement. On 18 Jul 2026 the Supreme Court recommended Parliament amend the IBC to give small operational creditors a fairer repayment mechanism.
- **Source:** MSME Samadhaan dashboard — https://samadhaan.msme.gov.in ; Lok Sabha reply — https://sansad.in/getFile/loksabhaquestions/annex/187/AU972_akHtxD.pdf ; LiveLaw (18 Jul 2026) — https://www.livelaw.in/supreme-court/supreme-court-recommends-ibc-amendment-to-protect-msmes-says-small-operational-creditors-need-fairer-repayment-mechanism-541890

### 2.5 Labour-code compliance and payroll costs
- **What:** The four Labour Codes came into force **21 November 2025**, replacing 29 central laws — a step-change in small employers' statutory burden.
- **Evidence/magnitude:** ESIC became pan-India and voluntary only below 10 employees; the Code's new "wages" definition (basic + DA, exclusions capped at 50%) pulls employees with gross pay above the ₹21,000 threshold back into ESIC coverage (**secondary-sourced; no official quantification found**). The **EPFO wage ceiling rose ₹15,000→₹25,000/month from 17 Sept 2026**, adding >51 lakh employees. Wage inflation stayed near 9% (Deloitte: 9.1% projected for 2026) yet **80% of Indian employers reported difficulty finding skilled talent in 2025** (ManpowerGroup) — scarcity, not affordability, drives the pain.
- **Why it amplified:** Anxiety was chronic (codes passed 2019–20, unimplemented); the Nov 2025 notification converted it into live, dated obligations with 2026 escalation (EPFO ceiling).
- **Source:** PIB/ESIC (21 Nov 2025) — https://dcdelhi.esic.gov.in/attachments/newseventfile/Government_Announces_Implementation_of_Four_Labour_Codes_to_Simplify_and_Streamline_Labour_Laws_English_1764057177.pdf ; KPMG Flash Alert (Sep 2026) — https://kpmg.com/xx/en/our-insights/gms-flash-alert/2026/flash-alert-2026-249.html ; ET (23 Jan 2025) — https://m.economictimes.com/jobs/hr-policies-trends/employers-expect-cautious-hiring-in-mar-qtr-80-pc-cos-facing-talent-scarcity-survey/articleshow/117480988.cms

### 2.6 Energy and fuel costs at the small-firm level
- **What:** Commercial/industrial power tariffs and diesel costs, and most SMBs' inability to access the rooftop-solar subsidy ladder.
- **Evidence/magnitude:** Commercial tariffs kept rising (e.g., Tamil Nadu +3.3% to ₹9.40/kWh from Jul 2025 — **secondary-sourced example**); CSEP (2024) found India has one of the world's largest commercial-vs-residential tariff spreads. Diesel prices were frozen from April 2022 until May 2026, then rose ~₹7.50/litre within weeks; CRISIL found truck operators could pass on only ~20% of the increase, leaving pre-EMI cash at ~21.6% of costs (Apr 2026). PM Surya Ghar crossed 50 lakh households and 14.8 GW (Aug 2026), but its central subsidy is residential-only — MSMEs rely on state schemes and CAPEX/RESCO models.
- **Why it amplified:** The 2026 fuel unfreeze ended a four-year cost shield, and the solar boom is visibly passing small businesses by.
- **Source:** PIB (Aug 2026) — https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2294208&reg=3&lang=1 ; PIB (27 Mar 2026) — https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2245970 ; CSEP (2024) — https://csep.org/working-paper/a-granular-comparison-of-international-electricity-prices-and-implications-for-india

## 3. Needs/Anxieties That Have COOLED/DECLINED, 2024→2026

### 3.1 Coercive GST burden: fear of arrest, prosecution, and rate chaos
- **What:** Fear of criminal exposure, crushing penalties, and an incomprehensible four-tier rate structure.
- **Evidence/magnitude:** The 56th Council (3 Sept 2025) collapsed rates into **5%/18% slabs plus a 40% demerit rate**, exempted GST on individual term-life and health insurance, and enabled auto-registration covering ~96% of new applicants (from 1 Nov 2025). The 57th Council (8 Oct 2026) went further: **withdrawal of GST arrest powers (omission of s.69), prosecution threshold raised ₹1 cr→₹5 cr, general penalty cut ₹25,000→₹10,000, late-fee waiver for filers with turnover ≤₹5 cr, simplified registration for small e-commerce sellers (Rule 14B)**, and an in-principle ARQP scheme (one annual return + quarterly payments) for B2C taxpayers ≤₹5 cr. Composition-scheme uptake **fell from 14.79 lakh (Jun 2025) to 13.55 lakh (Jun 2026)** — small traders migrating to the simplified mainstream rather than hiding. 2024's retrospective-demand panic (mass s.73 notices for FY 2017-18) had been answered by the Section 128A amnesty, effective 1 Nov 2024.
- **Why it cooled:** Revenue buoyancy (gross GST ₹22.27 lakh crore in FY26), the "GST 2.0" political framing, and automation reducing reliance on coercion.
- **Source:** PIB 56th Council (3 Sept 2025) — https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2163555 ; PIB 57th Council (8 Oct 2026) — https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2320934&lang=1&reg=48 ; GSTN 9-year statistical report — https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/offlineutilities/gst_statistics/9YearsReport.pdf

### 3.2 ONDC as a must-adopt channel anxiety
- **What:** The 2022–24 anxiety (and hope) that every SMB must get "ONDC-ready" or miss the open-commerce wave.
- **Evidence/magnitude:** ONDC retail orders peaked at 6.5M in Oct 2024, then slid to ~3.7–3.8M by mid-2025; participant incentives were cut ~90% by late 2024; the CEO stepped down in April 2025 with no permanent successor; ONDC **stopped publishing monthly retail numbers from Sept 2025**. In 2026 it repositioned ("ONDC 2.0") toward logistics, mobility and B2B kirana procurement (DigiDukaan, Feb 2026), raising ₹430 cr from Zoho, Uber, Paytm and others. **No credible report says ONDC formally shut its retail network — the correct characterisation is quiet de-emphasis.**
- **Why it cooled:** Subsidy-driven volumes were never self-sustaining; buyer apps (Pincode, Paytm) exited; the consumer-retail thesis lost institutional sponsorship.
- **Source:** Business Standard (27 Mar 2025) — https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/ondc-retail-decline-incentive-cuts-service-fee-growth-mobility-logistics-125032700419_1.html ; Moneycontrol (Aug 2025) — https://www.moneycontrol.com/technology/ondc-extends-financial-incentives-through-september-as-retail-orders-continue-to-decline-article-13537323.html ; Mint (Sep 2026) — https://www.tradingview.com/news/moodys:01ecfec5131d2:0-ondc-rethinks-retail-shifts-focus-to-improving-logistics-and-mobility

### 3.3 Aggregate formal-credit scarcity
- **What:** "Will any bank lend to me at all?" — the post-2023 tight-money fear.
- **Evidence/magnitude:** MSME credit exposure grew from ₹31 lakh crore (Mar 2024) to ₹35.2 lakh crore (Mar 2025, +13%); the broader commercial-lending universe reached ₹67.5 lakh crore by Dec 2025 (+16%) and ₹65.8 lakh crore by Mar 2026 (+14%) under the revised ₹100-crore classification. Balance-level delinquencies hit a **five-year low of 1.8%**. CGTMSE guarantee cover doubled to ₹10 crore (Feb 2025, estimated to unlock ₹1.5 lakh crore of extra credit); RBI restored pre-Nov-2023 risk weights on NBFC exposures (Feb 2025); ULI went live (Aug 2024) as the "UPI moment for lending"; ECLGS 5.0 followed in 2026. **Counter-trend (why "aggregate" is the operative word): the credit gap held at ~₹30 lakh crore (SIDBI, May 2025), only 41% of registered MSMEs have ever accessed formal credit, and stress is concentrating in the smallest tickets (see §4.3).**
- **Why it cooled:** Macro easing, guarantee expansion, and digital rails (ULI, account-aggregator journeys) broadened supply faster than demand grew.
- **Source:** TransUnion CIBIL–SIDBI MSME Pulse, May 2025 — https://newsroom.transunioncibil.com/indias-msme-commercial-credit-portfolio-grew-13-yoy ; SIDBI (May 2025) — https://www.sidbi.in/uploads/Understanding_Indian_MSME_sector_Progress_and_Challenges_13_05_25_Final.pdf ; Financial Express (1 Feb 2025) — https://www.financialexpress.com/budget/budget-2025-credit-guarantee-cover-for-msmes-startups-enhanced-customised-credit-card-for-micro-units-to-be-launched-3733652

### 3.4 Payments-rail continuity panic (the Paytm shock)
- **What:** The early-2024 fear that a regulator could switch off a major payment instrument overnight.
- **Evidence/magnitude:** RBI's 31 Jan 2024 action against Paytm Payments Bank barred deposits/top-ups after 15 Mar 2024, and CAIT asked traders to switch off Paytm (Feb 2024). By 2026 the panic had cooled: Paytm's UPI share recovered from 6.7% (Mar 2025) to 8.1% (Sep 2026) via third-party-app migration and 13M+ soundboxes; even the cancellation of PPBL's banking licence (24 Apr 2026) caused no merchant disruption.
- **Why it cooled:** Migration paths existed; the UPI ecosystem absorbed the shock.
- **Source:** RBI (31 Jan 2024) — https://fidcindia.org.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/RBI-PAYTM-PAYMENTS-BANK-31-01-24.pdf ; Moneycontrol (Nov 2026) — https://www.moneycontrol.com/technology/paytm-s-upi-market-share-grows-by-one-percentage-point-to-8-percent-in-two-years-article-14078212.html ; RBI (24 Apr 2026) — https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=62621

## 4. Net New / Emerging Anxieties, 2025–2026

### 4.1 The end of free UPI (merchant pricing)
NPCI's September 2026 decision introduced the **first-ever UPI merchant discount rate — 0.4% on P2M transactions above ₹2,000 (capped at ₹300), ₹5 flat in essential sectors, small merchants exempt**. Only ~4–5% of transactions are affected but roughly two-thirds of value; implementation was deferred until after Diwali after CAIT objections. For 65 million merchants built on free rails, "what will acceptance cost next year?" is a genuinely new question. (Business Standard, 16 Sep 2026 — https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/npci-sets-0-4-fee-on-upi-merchant-payments-above-2-000-effective-oct-15-126091501061_1.html ; New Indian Express, 13 Oct 2026 — https://www.newindianexpress.com/business/2026/Oct/13/npci-defers-04-mdr-on-upi-transactions-above-rs-2000-until-after-diwali)

### 4.2 The AI capability gap
AI moved from buzzword to budget item: 94% of surveyed MSMEs acknowledge AI's growth potential (NASSCOM–Meta, 2025), yet India SME Forum's Dec 2025 survey found **53.8% of MSMEs have adopted at least one digital tool while 46.2% remain fully offline**. Meta/YouGov (Aug–Sep 2026) found 84% of Indian/APAC small businesses using AI but **86% needing more help/training**. The anxiety is skill-shaped, not yet displacement-shaped — no credible India-specific displacement-fear percentage was found. (https://nasscom.in/ai/ai-enablement/pdf/enablement-of-ai-for-msme-whitepaper.pdf ; https://indiasmeforum.org/digishaastra/assets/docs/Final-META-Report-Card-2025.pdf ; https://about.fb.com/news/2026/10/more-than-three-quarters-of-small-business-owners-say-ai-makes-them-more-productive-but-most-need-more-guidance)

### 4.3 The small-ticket credit squeeze (the underside of the credit boom)
Even as aggregate credit grew, the July 2026 MSME Pulse shows **new-to-credit share of entity originations falling from 52% (FY23) to 42% (FY26)**, sub-₹10 lakh exposure delinquencies at 5.8% (vs 5.1% a year earlier), and unsecured business-loan vintages from Mar 2025 showing 2.9x higher ever-90+DPD. First-time borrowers are being rationed precisely as lenders retreat from the riskiest small tickets. (TransUnion CIBIL MSME Pulse Jul 2026 — https://www.transunioncibil.com/lp/msme-pulse-jul-2026 ; ET BFSI, 3 Jul 2026 — https://bfsi.economictimes.indiatimes.com/articles/indias-msmes-and-the-expanding-commercial-credit-landscape-challenges-and-opportunities/132153888)

### 4.4 Marketplace seller-fee wars
The 2026 festive season brought simultaneous fee hikes: Amazon cancellation fees of 2–10% of order value plus closing-fee rises; Flipkart penalties of ₹30–90 for dispatch delays; Delhivery raising D2C shipping up to 10%. "Zero commission" expansions (Amazon below ₹1,000, offset by a ₹5 per-transaction fee) blur actual unit economics. Channel-cost management is now a year-round discipline for sellers. (Moneycontrol, 24 Aug 2026 — https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/startup/amazon-flipkart-hike-seller-fees-ahead-of-festival-season-as-e-com-battle-intensifies-14013910.html ; Mint, Feb 2026 — https://www.livemint.com/companies/amazon-india-zero-commission-policy-products-one-thousand-sellers-business-growth-11772360566023.html)

### 4.5 Compliance frontier: e-invoicing expansion onto purchases
The e-invoicing threshold has been unchanged at ₹5 crore since Aug 2023, but GSTN's Oct 2026 advisory adds two categories — RCM-DOM (domestic reverse-charge purchases) and RCM-IMP (imported services) — requiring IRN generation on self-invoices by taxpayers with turnover ≥₹5 crore, extending e-invoicing onto purchase transactions for the first time, with rollout dated by commentators to 1 Jan 2027. (A2Z Taxcorp, 23 Oct 2026 — https://a2ztaxcorp.net/gstn-adds-two-e-invoicing-categories-for-reverse-charge-purchases)

## 5. Synthesis: The Operating Pain Calendar of an Indian SMB Today

Formalisation has deepened everywhere — GST taxpayers grew from 66.5 lakh (2017) to 1.65 crore (May 2026), Udyam registrations passed 7.8 crore by Feb 2026, and UPI gave ~65 million merchants free acceptance rails — but the burden's *shape* has changed from episodic terror to chronic, calendarised vigilance.

The pain calendar now runs: **monthly**, GSTR-1/GSTR-3B filing self-reconciled against IMS/GSTR-2B (Rule 88C/88D mismatches trigger 7-day windows and can block the next filing), plus ESIC/EPFO contributions under re-scoped wage definitions; **quarterly**, QRMP instalments (the ARQP direction promises relief) and marketplace fee/penalty cycles around festival quarters; **half-yearly**, MSME-1 filings for buyer-side late payments; **annually**, s.43B(h) reconciliation of payments to registered micro/small vendors, Udyam updates, and composition/GSTR-9 returns. Layered on top are non-calendar threats: a quick-commerce competitor's 12-minute delivery promise, a scam call, a ransomware email, a tariff revision — and, newly, a price tag on digital acceptance itself.

Structurally, the 2024→2026 shift is from **"will the system let me operate?"** (credit rationing, retrospective tax terror, payments shutdowns — all eased) to **"can I keep up?"** — with demand capture (quick commerce), digital trust (fraud), unit costs (fees, labour, energy), and capability (AI, tooling) defining the new anxiety frontier. The smallest firms sit at the sharp end of every trend: MSME logistics costs run to 16.9% of output versus 7.97% of GDP nationally (NCAER/DPIIT, Sept 2025), they are excluded from the residential-biased solar subsidy ladder, and they are where credit stress concentrates.
